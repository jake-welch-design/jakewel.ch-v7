<br><br>
I've been reflecting a lot these past couple weeks on friendship and on feelings of loneliness and belonging. Just this last weekend, I had the complete opposite experience to this weekend as Steph was away in Georgia for a work summit and I was left home alone for several days. Being alone is admittedly something I'm not very good at. Between being a Mormon missionary straight out of high school and getting married incredibly young, it's not something I've had much experience with. I thought it might be a good opportunity to practice, and I made every effort to enjoy myself. I visited the Brooklyn Museum (which I was honestly disappointed with, maybe I'll say more about that another time), sat in coffee shops, and read my book at the park. In the end, I was still left with a feeling of restlessness. The reality is, although I consider myself to be an introvert, I am very social, and spending time deep in conversation with people provides me with endless amounts of energy and fulfillment. 
<br><br>
Contrast that experience with this weekend.
When moving to a new place, one is forced to start over with a small circle of friends. Existing friendships become long distance, and in your new home you either meet new people or remain alone. We've been incredibly lucky to connect  with new folks in New York fairly easy, mostly through other friends, Utah transplants, and BYU creatives. Steph and I have both said many times that this is the most socially fulfilled we've both felt in a long time. But with my oldest friends visiting, I was reminded of how important it is to spend quality time with people who know you so deeply, your guard can come down entirely. Showing them my favorite places, visiting The Met, going to the Knicks watch party together at MSG (which was awesome by the way, Knicks in 4!) and having many great conversations together, I felt parts of myself that had been dormant for a long time being awakened. It can be lonely being away from the people who have known you through so many phases life. I needed that time together and feel more like myself now because of it! I am infinitely grateful for all my friends old and new (I know you're reading this) and for the richness you bring to my life. Because of you, I am never alone.
