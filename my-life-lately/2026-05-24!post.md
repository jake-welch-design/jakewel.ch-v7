Hi everyone, I hope you're enjoying the Memorial Day weekend. Unfortunately for us Brooklynites, it's been incredibly rainy and cold all weekend. We've had 14 Notify NYC texts sent in the last week warning of heavy rains, flooding, rip tides, and all manner of danger to be aware of because of the storms. This weather is obviously expected for spring, but I can't help but feel incredibly antsy because of it. Today I absolutely <i>had</I> to get out of the house, so I walked up to Berkley Street and 7th to Café Regular and read my book for a few hours, then afterwards stopped in at my favorite bookstore "Troubled Sleep", and made my way home through Grand Army and the park. I confess that I really enjoyed the misty, rainy ambience, and getting out felt great. Today would have been a good day to visit a museum and I nearly went to the Brooklyn Museum (I've still never been!), but I decided against it. I've been thinking a lot about art lately, (I wrote <a href="https://by.jakewel.ch/#2026-05-23">this essay</a> yesterday about why it moves me), and have been feeling the need to visit a museum ASAP. Maybe next weekend I'll go while Steph's away for her offsite in Savannah, GA!

I tend to be a homebody and I lean introverted in my personality, so it's easy to isolate myself. When we moved to New York, I knew that I couldn't let that happen, so I set a goal to always say yes when invited to something (assuming there aren't conflicts and I can make it work in my schedule). So far, I've mostly stuck to it, and it's meant pushing through feelings of being tired, lazy, nervous, socially anxious, and even sometimes going to one thing and hopping over to another afterwards. In any case, I'm yet to regret it. Last week a friend  invited me to a reading event their partner was hosting in the small basement of a bar in the East Village. I had a long work day and was coming off a stressful start to the week, and it was hot as hell. Still, I said yes knowing it would be a good time and I had such a great evening chatting with friendly and interesting new people! I enjoyed listening to all the talented (and very funny!) writers share their work with the small crowd, and I never would have experienced it had I given in to my trepidations. By saying yes, I've made some of my most interesting memories, it's lead to experiences I wouldn't have sought out on my own, and great conversations with people I wouldn't have normally crossed paths with.
