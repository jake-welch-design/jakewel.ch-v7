Warbler season has come and (nearly) gone here in New York. Prospect Park is a migration hotspot for around 36 species that stop in April-May while flying from South America to Greenland. Earlier this week, Steph and I went on a walk around the East side of the Quaker Cemetery in the park, and the woods were alive with birdsong. Opening Merlin (a bird identification and logging app, for those who are unaware) and hitting record immediately populated it with over a dozen colorful new bird species that we had never logged before. Later in the week, I went out with a friend to bird watch, and we saw a Scarlet Tanager, a bright red bird he's wanted to see for years, hopping around in the trees. This morning, I grabbed my coffee and binoculars and hit the park by 7:40 AM in an attempt to catch a few more, but it seems like they've finally begun their journey North, because I didn't see very many. Still, the real success of the morning was that I was in a quiet, green, beautiful place so early in the day. The Wood Thrushes and Northern Waterthrushes were singing loudly, and I was reminded that the real joy of bird watching is being present and aware of everything around you in nature.

Last month, I was following a friend around downtown Brooklyn in search of dinner and was surprised by how lost I felt despite frequently being in that neighborhood. He observed, "Sometimes it's nice to let someone else lead the way. They can help you break the monotony of your usual routes." There's wisdom to be found in that statement, and last week I had a few moments that reminded me of its truth. Someone reached out (via email, which I love!) to meet me for coffee at a place that's only 10 minutes away from my apartment, and the area it's in is one I don't ever go to. To be honest, I didn't think there was anything interesting over there! The place was a small corner diner, not too crowded, with good food and good prices; it's exactly the kind of diner I've been wanting to find since we moved to New York, and it was right next to us this entire time. Later that same day, I met up with a friend for coffee in Crown Heights at a place near the Brooklyn Museum. I go to Grand Army Plaza almost every day, but somehow I've never ventured down Eastern Parkway past the library. I've never even seen the museum from the front! I was shocked at how beautiful the neighborhood was. Even now, after living here for almost two years, I'm discovering new things. Sometimes I just need someone to lead the way.
