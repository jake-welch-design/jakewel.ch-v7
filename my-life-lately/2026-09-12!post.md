Hi friends and family! 
I hope everyone is enjoying the weather where they are now that we have drifted into late summer and we get closer to autumn. It is September 12, which means 2 days ago, Steph and I hit our 2 year mark of living in New York. Two years still feels like such a small amount of time when said out loud, but we feel so settled that it's hard to imagine how our lives would feel anywhere else. It's strange to have our New York anniversary coincide with 9/11, especially when we live in a neighborhood of Irish Catholics. For those who are unaware, Irish Catholics are one of the primary demographics in NYC who are firemen, policemen, EMTs, etc. The history for this is long and interesting and you should<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Americans_in_New_York_City">read about it</a>. Reportedly "40% of the 343 firefighters who died [on September 11th] had Irish ancestry." Because of this, our neighborhood feels different from the rest of the city every 9/11. Our cross street is even named for a firefighter who grew up in the neighborhood and died on 9/11.

This time of year is so incredible though. It feels like the city comes back alive after the heat of summer. Take note if you're reading this and don't already live here, September is the BEST time to come visit. 

I’ve recently had an exciting development regarding my <a href="https://restroomarchive.com">Restroom Archive</a> project. Anyone who has spent time around me knows that I have a habit of 3D scanning restrooms and have amassed quite a collection. The Restroom Archive has been my go-to passion project to build around as I've challenged myself to learn new skills in web development. A year and a half ago, it started as a simple website to view my collection, but as people discovered my work, it became what they remembered me for, and I realized this concept had potential. I wanted to build a website that would honor the collection as a legitimate archive; one that could do everything I dreamed. With my latest update, I expanded the functionality of the site to allow user submissions, commenting, and with more advanced navigation and documentation tools. I put months of work into this, mostly just wanting to make something I'm proud of, but not expecting many people to see it. 

As soon as the new version went live, I made a post about it on Instagram notify my friends. The response was positive, so <a href="https://www.instagram.com/reel/DctiYLTyGzB/?utm_source=ig_web_copy_link&stkn=MzRlODBiNWFlZA==">I made a reel</a> to guide others on how to submit their own scans–and it absolutely blew up. As of right now, the reel has 1.7 million views, 125K likes, and 1100 comments. At first the attention was exciting, and then stressful when (unfounded) accusations started coming in that the archive's a ruse to steal people's data and train AI. Immediately any gaps in my website revealed themselves, and I spent the next two days resolving them to make my site more robust and transparent. Now that the dust has settled, the Restroom Archive has over 350 users! <a href="https://restroomarchive.com/r/2026-08-31-tri-cities-airport-basement">The first user submission I received</a> came out of left field. I expected a decrepit bar restroom, or a highway pit stop. Not a ruined dirt room in an airport basement. In fact, at first I thought it was a joke until I saw the ghosts of two wall urinals in the corner. Receiving this submission felt validating. Opening up my collection to the world diversified it in ways I never could have alone. It's now been a week since the reel went viral, and I have received submissions from across the <a href="https://restroomarchive.com/r/2026-09-04-indigenous-roots">U.S.</a>, <a href="https://restroomarchive.com/r/2026-09-06-icelandic-aviation-museum">Iceland</a>, <a href="">Norway</a>, <a href="https://restroomarchive.com/r/2026-09-11-caf-eiles">Austria</a>, <a href="https://restroomarchive.com/r/2026-09-01-peadar-browns">Ireland</a>, <a href="https://restroomarchive.com/r/2026-09-02-faculty-of-the-university-of-fine-arts-stall">Spain</a>, and <a href="https://restroomarchive.com/r/2026-09-03-mnbaq-baillarg-e-pavillon">Canada</a>. Users gave been very thoughtful with their descriptions and annotations. They've aligned with my vision, valuing the storytelling that I saw in these spaces that compelled me to document them in the first place. 
<br><br>
Online discourse around the Restroom Archive has been overwhelming positive, too. Some have said that it reminds them of the early days of the internet when things still felt fun; that at first they were skeptical, but were blown away once they explored the site. <a href="https://www.404media.co/the-man-on-a-quest-to-digitally-preserve-americas-public-restrooms/">404 Media interviewed even interviewed me!</a> It's hard to describe how surreal, yet gratifying this experience has been. It confirms to me that, despite the project obviously having its quirks, its potential was real, and I'm very proud of it!
